03

A strategy I built and evolved

A real project from my time at Toddle
Real project, Toddle content library

The business problem

Toddle was launching a new content library for teachers, but the existing resources were not structured to support self service. New users, especially teachers just starting onboarding, had no clear path to the right content without asking support directly.

Research

I reviewed how users were actually moving through the product and found that most reference material had no consistent structure. Recorded product webinars, in particular, were sitting unused after their live session ended, with no path back into the product experience.

Strategy

I built a plan to give the library real content architecture: a schema ready structure across resources, contextual FAQs placed right where users needed them, and a repurposing pipeline so every webinar generated a family of reusable content instead of one time use.

Execution

I implemented the schema ready structure across the resource library, added contextual FAQs to the right panel of each resource, and turned recorded webinars into checklist articles, blogs, teacher playbooks, and summaries. I also built a dedicated glossary page for new teachers in the onboarding stage, and wrote the landing page and event content for the launches themselves.

Results

New teachers had a structured, self service path through the library from day one instead of relying on live support. Webinar content kept generating value long after the live session ended, instead of disappearing once it aired.

Key learning

Good content architecture has to be planned before a library scales, not retrofitted after. Structure is what turns a single piece of content into several, and what makes a resource easy to find again later.

04

Preparing content for AI powered discovery

SEO and AEO, treated as one discipline

Entity optimization

Naming people, products, and concepts clearly so AI systems can confidently identify what a page is about.

Schema markup

Structured data, especially FAQPage, used only where content genuinely matches the format.

Topical authority

Clusters that fully cover a subject's question tree, not single pages hoping to rank alone.

Citation potential

Direct, extractable answers near the top of a section, readable correctly even quoted out of context.

Structured content & FAQs

Clear heading hierarchy and genuine FAQs anticipating what a buyer would ask an AI assistant.

E-E-A-T

Real experience and expertise in the content itself: credible sourcing, named authorship, specificity.

Improving content with strategy

A before and after rewrite
Illustrative sample
Before

"In today's fast paced digital landscape, businesses are constantly looking for ways to improve their online presence. One important aspect many companies overlook is content structure. There are many factors that go into a strong content strategy, and in this article we'll explore some of them..."

After

What is a content strategy framework? A repeatable process a team uses to turn business goals into published content, covering research, planning, execution, and measurement. Most frameworks fail at one of two points: skipping audience research, or never returning to measurement to adjust the plan.

The original opens with throat clearing an AI system can't extract as a direct answer. The rewrite leads with the exact question a reader would ask, answers it immediately, and adds a specific, useful detail. Clarity and citation readiness improved together.

10

Continuous improvement, and where this is headed

The biggest shift in how I think about content strategy isn't a new tactic, it's a change in who counts as "the reader." A meaningful share of buyer decisions now happen inside an AI system before a human lands on a page, which makes structure and trustworthiness strategic decisions, not formatting details.

Content strategy and technical SEO are going to keep merging with something closer to structured data engineering. The strategists who stay valuable will be fluent in both the story and the schema.
